# What this taught me

I trained the one-layer transformer from Nanda et al. on modular addition mod 113 on my Mac: 30% of the data, weight decay 1.0, one seed, 50,000 epochs, about 46 minutes. The run showed the paper's main phenomenon: the model memorized the training data early, test accuracy stayed near zero for thousands of epochs, then rose to near-perfect between roughly epochs 8,600 and 9,600. I also ran the paper's mechanistic checks against my weights using my own implementations of its analyses, and they held. This covers one seed and my reimplementations of the analyses, not a full reproduction of the paper.

While test accuracy was flat, the loss restricted to the key frequencies kept falling, and the loss through the remaining frequencies rose and peaked late. The paper's reading is a memorizing component carrying the training set while a structured component matures, with grokking as the handover. My curves match that reading; two curves alone don't prove it.

The analysis found five key frequencies: 14, 15, 35, 42, and 52. The paper's model had 14, 35, 41, 42, 52, so four of five match and the exact set depends on the trained weights. In the ablation, keeping only the five key frequencies still solved the task, and removing them dropped the model below uniform guessing. Removing any single key frequency gave a test loss between 0.0085 and 0.228; removing the strongest non-key frequencies left test loss around 0.00020, close to the unablated 2.02e-04. On this seed, the generalizing solution leans almost entirely on those five frequencies. Whether they are the whole algorithm, I can't say.

Since this is one trajectory: test loss spiked hard early, and after the main cleanup the Gini coefficient and weight norm kept oscillating and never settled flat. The broad shapes match the paper's averaged curves. Two short pilot runs with the same seed also drifted apart within a few hundred epochs on my machine. The MPS backend doesn't guarantee deterministic ops, but I can't prove that caused the drift, and a second full run might not retrace this one.

The original ablation chart used a linear axis and the small bars were invisible. The differences that carry the argument only showed up on a log axis.
